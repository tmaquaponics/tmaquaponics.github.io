### Overview

Fry are super fragile and require prestine water for their first weeks. Instead of performing multiple water changes for each fry container, we will build a custom system where water is filtered in a main tank and fed back to each container. The objective here is more survivol rates for the fry, more ease and less maintenance for us.

### The build

In our [fishroom in a rack design](https://tmaquaponics.github.io/2026/04/03/Building-nano-RAS.html){:target="_blank"}, we must have a system to rise up fry from their larval to a juvenal state. The most famous fry system is Dean Tweeddale' system [1], and that's exactly what we're gonna to build.

**Tools and equipment**

The selection of the shape and size of the containers is imortant since it will determine how we interact with the system. Prefer shallow containers.
We need a drill, an airline splitter, air valve connectors and a small water pump. The inlet of the air valve splitter must be big enough to be connected with the outlet of the pump using an airline.

**How the system works**

The system replicates our rack system at a small scale. Water is filtered in the main tank, pumped to each container then overflows back to the tank. What this system offers is to have a big body of water for the fry, which garentees a stable water parameters, and a small space for the fry, so they don't spend much energy swimming around and looking for food. The containers are easy to siphon and can be swapped in and out pretty easily.

Drill each container two holes near its rim. The holes will be plugged with sponges to allow water to pass through while blocking the fry from escaping.
Connect the water pump to the ailine splitter, and connect an air valve to each one of its ends like specified in the picture above. The airline valves will allow us to regulate the flow of water.

Install the containers above your tank and voilà! You now have a fry system!
